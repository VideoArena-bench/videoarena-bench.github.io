Abstract

Understanding and tracking a dynamic world are fundamental to visual intelligence. Existing video benchmarks provide limited insight into whether models can maintain a coherent understanding of an evolving world state. We introduce Video-Arena, a benchmark that turns competitive games into scalable, verifiable tests of how well models understand evolving world states from videos. It covers three task categories: perceiving critical events, interpreting state updates, and tracking the resulting states. Our pipeline can generate videos and aligned reference labels from game records at scale. We release a golden set of 180 tasks across 9 games and evaluate models under two protocols: native visual understanding and tool-assisted agentic analysis. The best-performing systems achieve F1 scores of only 13.2 and 51.0, respectively. Native models struggle to perceive events reliably, while tool-assisted systems improve event recovery but still struggle to interpret state updates and track the resulting states consistently over time.

      Benchmark

      Games as scalable, verifiable video tests

      Game records are replayed into videos, and every reference label is derived from the record itself, by a parser for event games or by an engine evaluator for board and card games. One adapter per game is all it takes to add a new arena.

      Strict tuple F1

      A prediction counts only if its time falls within ±3 s of a GT event and every field of the tuple (event, update, state) is correct. Greedy one-to-one matching.

      Visual protocol

      Native video understanding: frames sampled at 1 FPS, capped at 256 frames, a single answer in one pass.

      Agentic protocol

      Tool-assisted analysis inside a coding-agent harness: up to 200 turns and 2 hours of free exploration of the full video.

      Findings

      What the arena reveals

      13.2

      Native models barely perceive

      The best visual-protocol F1 is 13.2 (mean 4.6), while a temporal ceiling at the same 256-frame budget reaches 90.2. The frames contain the answer; the models miss it.

      7.2 → 51.0

      Tools help by structuring evidence

      With a harness, Claude-Opus-5.0 jumps from 7.2 to 51.0 and the mean rises to 20.9. The gain comes from organising evidence, not from seeing more images.

      1.00 vs ~0.6

      Seeing ≠ tracking

      On board and card games the best agent perceives nearly every move (T1 ≈ 1.00) yet tracks the resulting state far less reliably (T3 ≈ 0.52–0.68).

      14.6

      Dense arenas remain open

      DOTA 2 and StarCraft II are both perception- and reasoning-bound: even the best agent reaches only T1 0.52 / 0.36, and 14.6 F1 on SC2.
